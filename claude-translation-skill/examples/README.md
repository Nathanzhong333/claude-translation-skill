# Translation Examples

This document showcases real examples of translations produced using the Claude Translation Skill.

## Example 1: Literary Translation (English to Chinese)

**Source Text (English):**
> "It was the best of times, it was the worst of times, it was the age of wisdom, it was the age of foolishness..."
> — Charles Dickens, *A Tale of Two Cities*

**【Core Translation】**

那是最美好的时代，那是最糟糕的时代；那是智慧的年头，那是愚昧的年头……

**【Key Terms Explanation】**

1. **"It was the best/worst of times"**
   - Original structure: Parallel construction with contrast
   - Translation strategy: Preserved parallelism using "那是...那是..." structure
   - Cultural note: The rhythmic repetition mirrors Chinese classical prose style

2. **"age" vs "times"**
   - Original: "times" (时代) and "age" (年头) used for variation
   - Translation: Used "时代" and "年头" to maintain variety while being natural in Chinese
   - Alternative considered: Using "年代" for "age", but "年头" felt more colloquial

**【Background Context】**

This opening from Dickens' *A Tale of Two Cities* is one of the most famous in English literature. The repetitive structure with contrasts ("best/worst," "wisdom/foolishness") creates a sense of paradox and dramatic tension.

In Chinese translation, maintaining this parallelism was crucial. The translation uses "那是" (that was) repeatedly to mirror Dickens' anaphora, a device that works equally well in Chinese literary tradition. The choice of "年头" over more formal alternatives brings a slight colloquial touch that matches Dickens' accessible style.

## Example 2: Technical Translation (English to Japanese)

**Source Text (English):**
> "The API uses RESTful architecture and supports JSON and XML response formats. Authentication requires an API key in the header."

**【Core Translation】**

このAPIはRESTfulアーキテクチャを採用し、JSONおよびXMLのレスポンス形式をサポートしています。認証には、ヘッダーにAPIキーが必要です。

**【Key Terms Explanation】**

1. **"RESTful architecture"**
   - Translation: Kept as "RESTful" (loanword in katakana: RESTful)
   - Rationale: Standard technical term in Japanese IT industry
   - Note: "アーキテクチャ" (architecture) is also a standard loanword

2. **"API key"**
   - Translation: "APIキー" (API kī)
   - Rationale: Direct loanword, universally understood by target audience
   - Alternative: Could use "認証鍵" (authentication key) but "APIキー" is industry standard

**【Background Context】**

Technical documentation requires precision and adherence to industry standards. In Japanese IT contexts, many English technical terms are borrowed directly (RESTful, API, JSON, XML), written in katakana. Using these standard terms ensures clarity for the professional audience.

The translation maintains the concise, instructional tone of the original. The structure follows Japanese technical writing conventions: topic → description → requirement.

## Example 3: Marketing Copy (English to Spanish)

**Source Text (English):**
> "Unlock your potential. Our revolutionary app transforms the way you work, giving you more time for what matters."

**【Core Translation】**

Libera tu potencial. Nuestra app revolucionaria transforma tu manera de trabajar, dándote más tiempo para lo que realmente importa.

**【Key Terms Explanation】**

1. **"Unlock your potential"**
   - Original: Metaphor of "unlocking" (opening something locked)
   - Translation: "Libera" (free/release) instead of literal "desbloquea"
   - Rationale: "Libera" has stronger emotional impact in Spanish marketing

2. **"what matters"**
   - Translation: "lo que realmente importa" (what really matters)
   - Added "realmente" (really) for emphasis, common in Spanish persuasive writing
   - Maintains the emotional weight of the English phrase

**【Background Context】**

Marketing translation prioritizes emotional impact and cultural resonance over literal accuracy. The Spanish version uses "libera" (free/liberate) instead of a literal translation of "unlock" because liberation has powerful cultural and emotional connotations in Spanish-speaking markets.

The added "realmente" (really) in "lo que realmente importa" is a natural intensifier in Spanish that enhances the persuasive tone. This is an example of transcreation—adapting the message for maximum impact in the target culture.

## Example 4: Academic Translation (Chinese to English)

**Source Text (Chinese):**
> 本研究采用定性与定量相结合的方法，对100名受试者进行了为期六个月的跟踪调查。

**【Core Translation】**

This study employed a mixed-methods approach combining qualitative and quantitative methods, conducting a six-month longitudinal survey of 100 subjects.

**【Key Terms Explanation】**

1. **"定性与定量相结合"**
   - Literal: "Qualitative and quantitative combined"
   - Translation: "mixed-methods approach combining qualitative and quantitative methods"
   - Rationale: "Mixed-methods" is the standard academic term in English research methodology

2. **"跟踪调查"**
   - Literal: "Follow-up/tracking survey"
   - Translation: "longitudinal survey"
   - Rationale: "Longitudinal" is the precise academic term for studies over time

**【Background Context】**

Academic translation demands precision and use of established terminology. "Mixed-methods" is a specific term in research methodology that precisely captures "定性与定量相结合." Using this standard term ensures the translation is recognized by academic readers.

Similarly, "longitudinal survey" is more precise than "follow-up survey" or "tracking survey" for describing research conducted over an extended period. The translation maintains the formal academic tone and follows English academic writing conventions (e.g., "100 subjects" rather than "100 research participants," which is more concise).

## Example 5: Idiomatic Expression (Chinese to English)

**Source Text (Chinese):**
> 他对这件事心知肚明，却装作事不关己、高高挂起的样子。

**【Core Translation】**

He knew perfectly well what was going on, but he put on an air of detached indifference, as if it were none of his business.

**【Key Terms Explanation】**

1. **"心知肚明"**
   - Literal: "Heart knows, stomach clear"
   - Idiom meaning: Fully aware, crystal clear understanding
   - Translation: "knew perfectly well"
   - Rationale: English equivalent that captures the emphasis on complete awareness

2. **"事不关己，高高挂起"**
   - Literal: "Matter not concerns self, hang high high"
   - Idiom meaning: Attitude of not caring about things that don't directly affect oneself
   - Translation: "detached indifference, as if it were none of his business"
   - Rationale: Combined two elements to convey both the emotional distance and the attitude

**【Background Context】**

Chinese four-character idioms (成语, chengyu) and set phrases pose unique translation challenges. "心知肚明" uses body parts metaphorically—heart and stomach represent understanding. While interesting, a literal translation would confuse English readers.

"事不关己，高高挂起" comes from traditional Chinese wisdom, criticizing those who remain aloof from others' problems. The translation uses "detached indifference" to capture the emotional coldness and "as if it were none of his business" to explain the attitude explicitly. This dual approach ensures English readers understand both the behavior and its negative connotation.

## Example 6: Poetry Translation (English to French)

**Source Text (English):**
> "Shall I compare thee to a summer's day?
> Thou art more lovely and more temperate."
> — William Shakespeare, Sonnet 18

**【Core Translation】**

Dois-je te comparer à un jour d'été ?
Tu es plus belle et plus douce encore.

**【Key Terms Explanation】**

1. **"temperate"**
   - Literal meaning: Moderate in temperature, mild
   - Multiple meanings: Also means even-tempered, balanced
   - Translation: "douce" (gentle, sweet, mild)
   - Rationale: Captures both the climatic and personality dimensions while maintaining poetic rhythm

2. **"Shall I compare thee"**
   - Formal/archaic English: "Shall I" + "thee"
   - Translation: "Dois-je te" (informal "you")
   - Rationale: French doesn't have an equivalent archaic register; using informal maintains intimacy

**【Background Context】**

Shakespeare's Sonnet 18 uses Early Modern English ("thee," "thou," "art"), which sounds romantic and elevated to modern ears. French lacks an equivalent historical register that feels similarly poetic without being incomprehensible.

The translation opts for "tu" (informal you) rather than "vous" (formal you) because the sonnet is an intimate love poem. "Douce" for "temperate" works on multiple levels in French—it suggests both mild weather and gentle temperament, paralleling Shakespeare's double meaning.

The meter is adapted rather than strictly preserved, as French and English have different rhythmic structures. The focus is on maintaining the romantic, questioning tone and the imagery of comparing a beloved person to ideal summer weather.
